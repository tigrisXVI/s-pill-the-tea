Blistra pill box v0.5

Italiano · Source on GitHub

28 compartments: 4 doses a day for one week. Everything sits inside one shell with a hinged lid: a display and a big button on the front, and a cup below where the pills land. Drag to rotate, pinch (or scroll) to zoom.

Your pills

Pick a shape and a compartment and add it. Then bring the compartment to the landing with “Next dose” and press the big button: the scale weighs it, the door lifts, the plate vibrates and the pills slide into the cup.

How it works

  1. The blue floor does not move. Around the circle it rises 10 mm like a spiral ramp, and it tilts toward the outer wall.
  2. The motor turns the hub. The 29 paddles turn with it but can slide up and down freely: they rest on the floor and climb with it.
  3. Each paddle pushes its compartment's dose up the ramp to the orange landing, exactly one compartment wide.
  4. The landing is a plate resting on a load cell: the box weighs the dose and compares it with the expected weight.
  5. At dose time the box beeps, the light bar blinks yellow and the display says it is time. The person presses the big button: a small servo lifts the door, whose skirt uncovers the opening in the wall. The plate vibrates and the pills slide down the chute and drop into the cup.
  6. It weighs again: if the plate is empty, the dose was delivered. The servo lowers its lever and the door closes under its own weight, so it cannot pinch fingers. The carousel only turns with the door closed.

Try it: add a few pills, press “Next dose” until their compartment arrives, then “Press the big button”: the pills slide down and drop into the cup. “See inside” shows the mechanism, “Show the electronics” the boards, screws and wires, and “Open the lid” is how you refill it.

The parts

    Open-source prototype, so far tested only in software. Pills fall with simplified physics here (gravity, friction, bounces, pill-to-pill contacts: they slide like discs, they don't roll); the more accurate simulation is the Blender one in the repository.
